# Where these faces came from

Every image in this folder was generated by a text-to-image model. **None of
them is a photograph of a real person**, and no real person's likeness was
scraped, collected or reused to build this library.

That was a deliberate choice. The game needs photorealistic faces to borrow
features from, and the obvious way to get them — pulling portraits of real
people off the web — would mean shipping identifiable strangers' faces, without
their knowledge, into an app that chops those faces up. Synthetic faces give the
same realism with nobody's consent to worry about.

## Source

Images are crops from the **SFHQ-T2I dataset** (Synthetic Faces High Quality –
Text2Image), by David Beniaguev:

- <https://github.com/SelfishGene/SFHQ-T2I-dataset>
- MIT License, © 2024 David Beniaguev
- The dataset's own description: *"there are no privacy issues or license issues
  surrounding these images"* because all of them are synthetically generated
  (Flux1, SDXL and DALL·E 3 outputs, curated).

Specifically, they are cut from the contact-sheet figures published in that
repository under `figures/`, then aligned and cropped by the scripts in
`../tools/`.

## What was done to them

1. `tools/slice_montages.py` — cut each contact sheet into individual images.
2. `tools/build_stock_faces.py` — ran MediaPipe Face Landmarker over each one,
   rejected anything turned away, rolled, too small or partly out of frame, and
   wrote an aligned square crop plus the feature rectangles in `manifest.json`.
3. `tools/curate_stock.py` — hand-picked the faces that make good spare parts:
   adults only, eyes unobstructed, calm expressions, even lighting, and no
   vividly dyed hair (a streak of electric blue in one of six eyebrows gives the
   answer away).

`manifest.json` holds, per face, the `rel` rectangles for each feature and an
average skin tone. Both are precomputed so the app needs no face detection to
use them.
